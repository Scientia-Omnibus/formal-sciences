In this section we will see how to convert among different types of units, such as feet to miles or kilograms to pounds. The basic idea in all of the unit conversions will be to use a form of <span class="math-inline">1,</span> the multiplicative identity, to change the units but not the value of a quantity.


## Make Unit Conversions in the U.S. System

There are two systems of measurement commonly used around the world. Most countries use the metric system. The United States uses a different system of measurement, usually called the U.S. system. We will look at the U.S. system first.

The U.S. system of measurement uses units of inch, foot, yard, and mile to measure length and pound and ton to measure weight. For capacity, the units used are cup, pint, quart and gallons. Both the U.S. system and the metric system measure time in seconds, minutes, or hours.

The equivalencies among the basic units of the U.S. system of measurement are listed in . The table also shows, in parentheses, the common abbreviations for each measurement.


In many real-life applications, we need to convert between units of measurement. We will use the identity property of multiplication to do these conversions. We’ll restate the Identity Property of Multiplication here for easy reference.


<div class="math-display">
For any real number a, a· 1=a 1· a=a
</div>


To use the identity property of multiplication, we write <span class="math-inline">1</span> in a form that will help us convert the units.
For example, suppose we want to convert inches to feet.
We know that <span class="math-inline">1</span> foot is equal to <span class="math-inline">12</span> inches, so we can write <span class="math-inline">1</span> as the fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>1 ft</sup>⁄<sub>12 in</sub>.</span> When we multiply by this fraction, we do not change the value but just change the units.


But <span class="math-inline"><sup>12 in</sup>⁄<sub>1 ft</sub></span> also equals <span class="math-inline">1.</span> How do we decide whether to multiply by <span class="math-inline"><sup>1 ft</sup>⁄<sub>12 in</sub></span> or <span class="math-inline"><sup>12 in</sup>⁄<sub>1 ft</sub>?</span> We choose the fraction that will make the units we want to convert *from* divide out.
For example, suppose we wanted to convert <span class="math-inline">60</span> inches to feet.
If we choose the fraction that has inches in the denominator, we can eliminate the inches.


<div class="math-display">
60 · <sup>1 ft</sup>⁄<sub>12</sub>=5 ft
</div>

On the other hand, if we wanted to convert <span class="math-inline">5</span> feet to inches, we would choose the fraction that has feet in the denominator.


<div class="math-display">
5 ft· <sup>12 in</sup>⁄<sub>1</sub>=60 in
</div>

We treat the unit words like factors and ‘divide out’ common units like we do common factors.


**Make unit conversions.**

- Multiply the measurement to be converted by <span class="math-inline">1;</span> write <span class="math-inline">1</span> as a fraction relating the units given and the units needed.

- Multiply.

- Simplify the fraction, performing the indicated operations and removing the common units.

## Make Unit Conversions in the Metric System


In the metric system, units are related by powers of <span class="math-inline">10.</span> The root words of their names reflect this relation.
For example, the basic unit for measuring length is a meter.
One kilometer is <span class="math-inline">1000</span> meters; the prefix *kilo-* means thousand.
One centimeter is <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>100</sub></span> of a meter, because the prefix *centi-* means one one-hundredth (just like one cent is <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>100</sub></span> of one dollar).

Since the **metric system** is based on multiples of ten, conversions involve multiplying by multiples of ten. In Decimal Operations, we learned how to simplify these calculations by just moving the decimal.

To multiply by <span class="math-inline">10,100,or 1000,</span> we move the decimal to the right <span class="math-inline">1,2,or 3</span> places, respectively. To multiply by <span class="math-inline">0.1,0.01,or 0.001</span> we move the decimal to the left <span class="math-inline">1,2,or 3</span> places respectively.

We can apply this pattern when we make measurement conversions in the metric system.

In , we changed <span class="math-inline">3200</span> grams to kilograms by multiplying by <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>1000</sub> (or 0.001).</span> This is the same as moving the decimal <span class="math-inline">3</span> places to the left.
