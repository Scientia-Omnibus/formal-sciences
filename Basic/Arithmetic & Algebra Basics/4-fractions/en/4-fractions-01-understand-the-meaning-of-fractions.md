## Understand the Meaning of Fractions

Andy and Bobby love pizza. On Monday night, they share a pizza equally. How much of the pizza does each one get? Are you thinking that each boy gets half of the pizza? That’s right. There is one whole pizza, evenly divided into two parts, so each boy gets one of the two equal parts.

In math, we write <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> to mean one out of two parts.


On Tuesday, Andy and Bobby share a pizza with their parents, Fred and Christy, with each person getting an equal amount of the whole pizza.
How much of the pizza does each person get?
There is one whole pizza, divided evenly into four equal parts.
Each person has one of the four equal parts, so each has <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub></span> of the pizza.


On Wednesday, the family invites some friends over for a pizza dinner. There are a total of <span class="math-inline">12</span> people. If they share the pizza equally, each person would get <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>12</sub></span> of the pizza.


**Fractions**


A fraction is a way to represent parts of a whole.
The denominator <span class="math-inline">b</span> represents the number of equal parts the whole has been divided into, and the numerator <span class="math-inline">a</span> represents how many parts are included.
The denominator, <span class="math-inline">b,</span> cannot equal zero because division by zero is undefined.

What does the fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>2</sup>⁄<sub>3</sub></span> represent? The fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>2</sup>⁄<sub>3</sub></span> means two of three equal parts.

## Model Improper Fractions and Mixed Numbers


In  (b), you had eight equal fifth pieces.
You used five of them to make one whole, and you had three fifths left over.
Let us use fraction notation to show what happened.
You had eight pieces, each of them one fifth, <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>5</sub>,</span> so altogether you had eight fifths, which we can write as <span class="math-inline"><sup>8</sup>⁄<sub>5</sub>.</span> The fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>8</sup>⁄<sub>5</sub></span> is one whole, <span class="math-inline">1,</span> plus three fifths, <span class="math-inline"><sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>5</sub>,</span> or <span class="math-inline">1<sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>5</sub>,</span> which is read as *one and three-fifths*.


The number <span class="math-inline">1<sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>5</sub></span> is called a mixed number. A mixed number consists of a whole number and a fraction.


**Mixed Numbers**

A **mixed number** consists of a whole number <span class="math-inline">a</span> and a fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>b</sup>⁄<sub>c</sub></span> where <span class="math-inline">c≠ 0.</span> It is written as follows.


<div class="math-display">
a<sup>b</sup>⁄<sub>c</sub> c≠ 0
</div>


Fractions such as <span class="math-inline"><sup>5</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub>,<sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>,<sup>5</sup>⁄<sub>5</sub>,</span> and <span class="math-inline"><sup>7</sup>⁄<sub>3</sub></span> are called improper fractions.
In an improper fraction, the **numerator** is greater than or equal to the **denominator**, so its value is greater than or equal to one.
When a fraction has a numerator that is smaller than the denominator, it is called a proper fraction, and its value is less than one.
Fractions such as <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>,<sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>7</sub>,</span> and <span class="math-inline"><sup>11</sup>⁄<sub>18</sub></span> are proper fractions.


**Proper and Improper Fractions**

The fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>a</sup>⁄<sub>b</sub></span> is a **proper fraction** if <span class="math-inline">a<b</span> and an **improper fraction** if <span class="math-inline">a≥ b.</span>

## Model Equivalent Fractions


Let’s think about Andy and Bobby and their favorite food again.
If Andy eats <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> of a pizza and Bobby eats <span class="math-inline"><sup>2</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub></span> of the pizza, have they eaten the same amount of pizza?
In other words, does <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>=<sup>2</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub>?</span> We can use fraction tiles to find out whether Andy and Bobby have eaten *equivalent* parts of the pizza.


**Equivalent Fractions**

**Equivalent fractions** are fractions that have the same value.

Fraction tiles serve as a useful model of equivalent fractions. You may want to use fraction tiles to do the following activity. Or you might make a copy of  and extend it to include eighths, tenths, and twelfths.

Start with a <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> tile. How many fourths equal one-half? How many of the <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub></span> tiles exactly cover the <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> tile?

Since two <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub></span> tiles cover the <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> tile, we see that <span class="math-inline"><sup>2</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub></span> is the same as <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>,</span> or <span class="math-inline"><sup>2</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub>=<sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>.</span>

How many of the <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>6</sub></span> tiles cover the <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> tile?

Since three <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>6</sub></span> tiles cover the <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> tile, we see that <span class="math-inline"><sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>6</sub></span> is the same as <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>.</span>

So, <span class="math-inline"><sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>6</sub>=<sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>.</span> The fractions are **equivalent fractions**.

## Find Equivalent Fractions


We used fraction tiles to show that there are many fractions equivalent to <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>.</span> For example, <span class="math-inline"><sup>2</sup>⁄<sub>4</sub>,<sup>3</sup>⁄<sub>6</sub>,</span> and <span class="math-inline"><sup>4</sup>⁄<sub>8</sub></span> are all equivalent to <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>.</span> When we lined up the fraction tiles, it took four of the <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>8</sub></span> tiles to make the same length as a <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> tile.
This showed that <span class="math-inline"><sup>4</sup>⁄<sub>8</sub>=<sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub>.</span> See .


We can show this with pizzas, too. (a) shows a single pizza, cut into two equal pieces with <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> shaded. (b) shows a second pizza of the same size, cut into eight pieces with <span class="math-inline"><sup>4</sup>⁄<sub>8</sub></span> shaded.

This is another way to show that <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> is equivalent to <span class="math-inline"><sup>4</sup>⁄<sub>8</sub>.</span>


How can we use mathematics to change <span class="math-inline"><sup>1</sup>⁄<sub>2</sub></span> into <span class="math-inline"><sup>4</sup>⁄<sub>8</sub>?</span> How could you take a pizza that is cut into two pieces and cut it into eight pieces?
You could cut each of the two larger pieces into four smaller pieces!

## Key Concepts

- *Property of One*
  - Any number, except zero, divided by itself is one.
<span class="math-inline"><sup>a</sup>⁄<sub>a</sub>=1</span>, where <span class="math-inline">a≠ 0</span>.

- *Mixed Numbers*
  - A *mixed number* consists of a whole number <span class="math-inline">a</span> and a fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>b</sup>⁄<sub>c</sub></span> where <span class="math-inline">c≠ 0</span>.
  - It is written as follows: <span class="math-inline">a<sup>b</sup>⁄<sub>c</sub> c≠ 0</span>

- *Proper and Improper Fractions*
  - The fraction <span class="math-inline"><sup>a</sup>⁄<sub>b</sub></span> is a proper fraction if <span class="math-inline">a<b</span> and an improper fraction if <span class="math-inline">a≥ b</span>.

- *Convert an improper fraction to a mixed number.*
  - Divide the denominator into the numerator.
  - Identify the quotient, remainder, and divisor.
  - Write the mixed number as quotient<span class="math-inline"><sup>remainder</sup>⁄<sub>divisor</sub></span>.

- *Convert a mixed number to an improper fraction.*
  - Multiply the whole number by the denominator.
  - Add the numerator to the product found in Step 1.
  - Write the final sum over the original denominator.

- *Equivalent Fractions Property*
  - If <span class="math-inline">a, b,</span> and <span class="math-inline">c</span> are numbers where <span class="math-inline">b≠ 0</span>, <span class="math-inline">c≠ 0</span>, then <span class="math-inline"><sup>a</sup>⁄<sub>b</sub>=<sup>a· c</sup>⁄<sub>b· c</sub></span>.
